## 01 · The context

Parque de las Calabazas is a theme park celebrating autumn and Halloween: experiences, activities, music, and photo sets for the whole family.

I was hired as a freelancer to build the web experience for the 2026 edition (PDLC2026). The goal was clear: take the site to an immersive experience that reflected the magic of the physical event, and attract visitors from the very first screen.

The starting point was minimal. The previous year the site had three sections (Home, Amenities, and FAQ) for a single location. For 2026 the event was growing to two locations, so the site had to become more robust without becoming harder to use.

## 02 · My role

Design and development, end to end.

I worked as a freelancer with direct contact with the event's design team. They shared last year's multimedia assets and some supporting graphics. From there, I defined a single concept for the project, and with that concept clear, I created new graphics and multimedia elements for every section of the site.

Design and development in the same hands: I defined the experience and built it in HTML embedded on WordPress. That technical decision was deliberate - prioritize design without giving up the SEO that WordPress provides.

## 03 · The challenge

Immersion that feels clean, not overwhelming.

The client gave almost total creative freedom. The real challenge wasn't technical or about scope: it was about judgment. An immersive site can easily fall into excess - animations everywhere, heavy multimedia, an experience that's impressive in the first five seconds and unbearable in actual use.

My goal was the opposite: for the immersion to feel clean, modern, and easy to understand. I used UX and UI to optimize every screen, so the visuals attracted and the structure guided. The experience had to be unique, but above all intuitive.

---

## 04 · The architecture

From a single-location site to a two-location one, without doubling complexity for the user.

The previous year's site was linear: three sections, one location. For 2026, with two locations, the structure had to grow without losing clarity. I solved it with an architecture built around a central Home (a "Choose your location" landing) that branches into two sites: Parque Fundidora (Monterrey) and Foro Valle Oriente (San Pedro), each with its own Home, Amenities, and FAQ sections.

Each location has its own content, dates, pricing, and amenities, but they share identity and navigation. The user chooses their location from the start and enters a dedicated flow, without mixing information from both. Doubling the number of screens didn't mean doubling friction: each path feels simple because it only shows what's relevant to that location.

## 05 · The experience

Micro-animations, multimedia, and a journey that feels like the park.

With the concept defined, I built the experience layer: micro-animations that bring navigation to life, original multimedia per section, and a visual rhythm that carries the event's atmosphere onto the screen. Every graphic element was created new, aligned to the project's single concept, instead of reusing last year's material.

All under the same premise: animation guides, it doesn't distract. States respond, transitions have purpose, and visual weight never compromises load speed or comprehension.

Immersion and clarity aren't at odds. The trick is that every animation has a reason to exist.

## 06 · Current status

Site finished, awaiting launch.

The site went through its final adjustments and is ready. As a seasonal project, launch aligns with the start of the 2026 event, so there are no usage metrics yet.

What there is: a product delivered in 11 days - from concept to a complete, immersive site ready to welcome its first visitors. An express project that proves speed and design judgment aren't at odds.
