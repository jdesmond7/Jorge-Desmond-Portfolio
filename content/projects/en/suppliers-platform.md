## 01 · The problem

**Supplier information lived scattered across spreadsheets and email chains. Every quote was manual work, slow and error-prone.**

GroWrk manages supplier relationships across multiple regions, handling constantly changing data: pricing, product catalogs, regional availability, contacts, and wire transfer details. Before this initiative, all of it was fragmented across spreadsheets and manual email workflows.

The cost was operational and real. Limited visibility, slow response times to clients, and growing risk across the Logistics, Finance, and Operations teams. The lack of centralized data didn't just slow the team down. It limited the ability to scale into new regions.

The initial request was simple: centralize supplier data into an internal platform where teams could view, update, and manage information. But as we uncovered the real inefficiencies in the workflow, what began as a visibility tool became a core operational capability. And eventually, the foundation for a Supplier App and an API.

---

## 02 · My role

**I led the UX initiative end-to-end. I mentored a Junior designer, aligned the CEO, and held ownership of the system-level decisions.**

I led the UX initiative for the Suppliers platform, collaborating closely with the Product Owner, the CEO, Engineering, and operational stakeholders. I worked alongside a Junior UI Designer, distributing responsibilities strategically while holding the overall direction, the discovery process, and the system-level decisions.

My work broke into three fronts:

Stakeholder alignment. I worked directly with the Product Owner and the CEO to define scope and long-term opportunity. I structured and facilitated focus groups to uncover operational workflows and real pain points, translating the findings into a complete user flow.

Design direction. I guided the Junior designer through wireframing and high-fidelity design, mentoring throughout the process and ensuring alignment with business goals and system standards.

System and implementation. I developed the interactive prototype for validation and extended the Design System with new components for supplier management, documenting every change and working with Engineering toward a scalable implementation.

> Although responsibilities were distributed, I retained ownership of the strategic decisions, stakeholder communication, and final UX validation.

---

## 03 · Discovery

**I chose focus groups over 1:1 interviews. The operational complexity called for it.**

Given the complexity of supplier management, I decided to run structured focus groups (3 to 5 stakeholders per session) instead of only individual interviews. The reason: the group format generates dialogue, exposes hidden dependencies, and surfaces the undocumented workarounds that a one-on-one interview would miss.

Together we mapped the complete supplier workflow, identifying data touchpoints, manual tasks, repetitive processes, bottlenecks in quote preparation, and error-prone areas.

![growrk-suppliers-userflow.png](/images/projects/growrk-suppliers-platform/growrk-suppliers-userflow.png)

This collaborative discovery phase created alignment across stakeholders and ensured the solution reflected real operational needs, not assumptions.

## 04 · Ideation and design

**From wireframes to a decision that changed the scope of the project.**

With the workflow defined, I started exploring structural solutions. The low-fidelity wireframes were created collaboratively: the Junior designer translated the workflow into early layout concepts while I guided structure, prioritization, and scalability decisions.

Before moving to high fidelity, we ran a working session with Engineering to validate technical feasibility and identify automation opportunities. That's where the most important decision of the project came up.

**The automated invoicing tool**

The initial scope focused on viewing and updating data. But while mapping the flow, I saw a pain point nobody had put on the table: the team drafted each quote by hand, in plain-text emails. Between 20 and 30 minutes per quote.

I proposed an automated invoice generation tool. It wasn't in the brief. I raised it because the data to build it already existed in the system we were creating: it just needed to be connected.

This required close collaboration with Engineering to define data dependencies, automation logic, permission structures, and scalability considerations. The result cut quote preparation from 20-30 minutes to 5-10.

![growrk-suppliers-wireframes-lowfi.png](/images/projects/growrk-suppliers-platform/growrk-suppliers-wireframes-lowfi.png)

> The best feature of the project wasn't in the original request. It came from understanding the flow, not from reading the brief.

## 05 · High fidelity and system

**The Junior executed the mockups. I made sure everything held up on the system.**

The Junior designer developed the high-fidelity mockups under my mentorship, while I ensured alignment with the evolving Design System, consistent component usage, clear information hierarchy, and accessibility and usability best practices.

I developed the interactive prototype used in the validation sessions and presentations. In parallel, I extended the Design System with new components for supplier management and documented every change so Engineering could implement them consistently.

## 06 · Validation and adoption

**Positive feedback. Initial resistance. And a complete shift in four weeks.**

To minimize bias, I ran individual walkthrough sessions with each stakeholder instead of group demos. The feedback was very positive, especially on clarity of data visibility, time savings, and reduction in manual effort.

But adoption faced resistance at first. The team had worked in spreadsheets for years and the habit was heavy. Within 3 to 4 weeks, the transition was complete. Not by mandate, but because they experienced the difference: quote preparation dropped from 20-30 minutes to 5-10, manual errors went down, and operational transparency improved.

That translated into roughly 2 to 3 hours saved daily at the team level.

> Resistance to change isn't beaten with arguments. It's beaten by letting people feel the savings in their own work.

## 07 · The next bet - Supplier App

**The internal success revealed a bigger opportunity. We chased it. It didn't work. And that's a result too.**

The internal platform served the operational teams. But seeing the efficiency gains and the structured data management, leadership identified an opportunity: extend that transparency outward, directly to the suppliers.

I worked end-to-end on the Supplier App, a platform for suppliers to manage and update their own catalogs. The goal was to decentralize data updates and reduce internal operational dependency. We prepared it over 8 months between design and engineering, and gave it a year in the suppliers' hands.

![growrk-supplier-app-screens.png](/images/projects/growrk-suppliers-platform/growrk-supplier-app-screens.png)

It didn't work. After several improvements they themselves requested, adoption never took off. We decided to shut it down.

The underlying reason was two things. First, there was no clear incentive: keeping their inventory on our platform was extra work with no direct benefit for them. Second, most already had their own system and saw no reason to migrate. The problem wasn't the design. It was the incentive structure of the ecosystem.

From a product perspective, it was a huge opportunity: building a solution from scratch, defining flows for a new user type, and validating assumptions about ownership, responsibility, and system integration. The product was shut down. The lesson wasn't.
