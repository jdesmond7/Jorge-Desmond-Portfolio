## 01 · The context

**In Mexico, more than 80% of payments under 500 pesos are still made in cash. SPIN by OXXO was born to change that, leaning on the trust people already had in the brand.**

SPIN by OXXO is a consumer fintech app launched in 2021. Its goal: bring financial services to a broad audience, including users with low digital and financial literacy, without sacrificing usability or regulatory compliance.

The challenge wasn't technological, it was about trust. For someone who has never used a digital bank, moving money from a phone is scary: what if I make a mistake?, what if I lose my money?, who do I complain to?

SPIN's advantage was structural: more than 20,000 OXXO stores as a physical safety net. The OXXO cashier, someone people already know and trust, was the product's emotional anchor. My job was to translate that physical trust into the digital environment, in the most sensitive action of all: sending money.

## 02 · My role

**Product Designer on a team of four. Owner of the SPEI flow end-to-end. Later, Design System Lead.**

I worked as a Product Designer on a team of four designers, each with ownership over specific flows for the initial launch. I led the SPEI transfers flow (national and international) from start to finish: hypothesis, competitive analysis, interaction design, user validation, and iteration toward the MVP.

In parallel, I contributed to FTUX, the Home dashboard, and Rewards & Promotions, keeping the overall experience consistent. As the product evolved, I was appointed Design System Lead, focused on defining and scaling reusable components with Product and Engineering.

## 03 · The process

**From hypothesis to MVP, validating every decision with real users.**

**Discovery.** The project kicked off with discovery alongside UX Research, the Product Owner, and Engineering, to understand the target users (many not digitally native) and identify trust barriers around moving money through an app. We defined User Personas from the start, not as a deliverable but as a constant reference. María Hernández: owner of an informal business in the State of Mexico, low-to-medium digital literacy, high relationship with OXXO. Her fear: making a mistake and losing money. Her need: understanding what happens at every step.

<!-- IMG: /images/projects/oxxo-spin/spin-persona-maria.png - María Hernández persona used as a design reference -->

**Defining the flow.** I ran a competitive analysis of national and international financial apps (web and mobile) to understand interaction patterns, regulatory constraints, and best practices. That analysis fed the design hypotheses: reduce cognitive load, make rules and limits explicit, reinforce control and transparency. I translated the hypotheses into detailed flow diagrams and aligned early with the Product Owner and the team to validate logic, edge cases, and constraints before visual design. That alignment reduced rework and resolved usability, compliance, and technical feasibility from the start.

**Validation and iteration.** I designed mid-fidelity wireframes to validate structure and logic internally. After feedback, I developed high-fidelity prototypes in two-week cycles, tested with real users in moderated 1:1 sessions alongside UX Research. We tested with ~50 users remotely across all of Mexico, with questions focused on trust, clarity, and usability: how safe they felt sending money, how often they would use the flow, and whether users with low digital literacy could complete the task without help.

The result: 90% acceptance. Users described the flow as clear, trustworthy, and easy to understand, regardless of age or digital experience.

## 04 · Key decisions

**The decisions that don't appear in the brief, but that defined the flow's trust.**

Designing for someone's money is different. A usability error in a notes app is an annoyance; in a transfer, it's the fear of losing what was hard to earn. I made these two decisions on my own, based on experience designing for real users. Both were central to the success of the testing.

**Showing the rules and limits before the amount, not after.** The common pattern in financial apps lets the user type the amount and, if it exceeds a limit, shows the error afterward. That frustrates: the person has already settled on sending a certain amount and the system rejects it. I flipped it. The rules, limits, and restrictions are shown before typing the amount. The user knows from the start how much they can send and under what conditions. For someone who already distrusts money apps, avoiding a single friction like this completely changes their sense of control.

**An extra confirmation screen before sending.** The number one fear was making a mistake and losing money. Not speed, not the number of steps: the irreversible error. I added an explicit confirmation before executing the transfer, where the user reviews recipient and amount before confirming. One more step in the flow, yes, but one that turns anxiety into reassurance. The OXXO brand already carried much of that trust; the flow reinforced it. In testing, users mentioned again and again that they felt safe. OXXO opened the door, the design confirmed they could walk through it.

## 05 · UI and system

**A system-first approach, because in fintech visual consistency is trust.**

With the flows validated, I moved into UI, producing development-ready high-fidelity mockups aligned with SPIN's brand and the Design System I was building at the time. The work was deliberately system-first: typography, color, spacing, and components that were consistent, reusable, and scalable. In fintech this is critical, because visual consistency and predictable interactions are part of how trust is built.

The interface was designed to feel clear, calm, and accessible, with special attention to states and feedback (loading, success, error, empty). Sensitive actions like SPEI transfers always came with clear visual cues and confirmations.

I built the Design System from scratch. It started small and grew with the product during launch. By aligning UI decisions with that system, the team reduced inconsistencies, streamlined handoff, and iterated faster. That approach laid the groundwork for SPIN to adapt to regulatory and functional changes over time.

## 06 · Designing to scale

**From day one, SPIN was designed with the long term in mind.**

SPIN was built on modular, flexible flows that allowed new capabilities (remittances, promotions, rewards, value-added features) to be introduced without increasing complexity for the user. Clarity and trust were preserved at every step, guided by a continuous focus on the behavior of the non-digitally-native user in Mexico.

That approach laid the groundwork for SPIN to grow while keeping an accessible and trustworthy experience, even as regulatory requirements and the product offering expanded after my departure.
