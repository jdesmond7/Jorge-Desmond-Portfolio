## 01 · The context

**In Mexico, more than 80% of payments under 500 pesos are still made in cash. SPIN by OXXO was born to change that, leaning on the trust people already had in the brand.**

SPIN by OXXO is a consumer fintech app launched in 2021. Its goal: bring financial services to a broad audience, including users with low digital and financial literacy, without sacrificing usability or regulatory compliance.

The challenge wasn't technological. It was about trust. For someone who has never used a digital bank, moving money from a phone is scary. What if I make a mistake? What if I lose my money? Who do I complain to?

SPIN's advantage was structural: more than 20,000 OXXO stores operating as a physical safety net. The OXXO cashier, someone people already know and trust, became the product's emotional anchor. My job was to translate that physical trust into the digital environment, especially in the most sensitive action of all: sending money.

## 02 · My role

**Product Designer on a team of four. Owner of the SPEI transfers flow end-to-end. Later, Design System Lead.**

I worked as a Product Designer within a team of four designers, each with ownership over specific flows for SPIN's initial launch.

I led the SPEI transfers flow (national and international) from start to finish: from hypothesis definition and competitive analysis to interaction design, user validation, and iteration toward the launch MVP.

In parallel, I contributed to other core areas of the product: FTUX, the Home dashboard, and Rewards & Promotions, keeping the overall experience consistent. As the product evolved, I was appointed Design System Lead, focused on defining and scaling reusable components in close collaboration with Product and Engineering.

> The SPEI flow wasn't just another screen. It was the product's trust acid test.

## 03 · The process

**From hypothesis to MVP, validating every decision with real users.**

### Discovery

The project kicked off with a discovery phase in close collaboration with the UX Research team, the Product Owner, and Engineering. The goal was to deeply understand the target users, many of them not digitally native, and identify the trust barriers around moving money through an app.

We defined User Personas from the start. Not as a deliverable, but as a constant reference. María Hernández, owner of an informal business in the State of Mexico, low-to-medium digital literacy, high relationship with OXXO. Her main fear: making a mistake and losing money. Her need: understanding what happens at every step.

![spin-persona-maria.png](/images/projects/oxxo-spin/spin-persona-maria.png)

Those personas grounded every design decision in real behaviors, not assumptions.

### Defining the flow

For the SPEI flow, I ran a competitive and comparative analysis of national and international financial apps, web and mobile. The goal was to understand common interaction patterns, regulatory constraints, and best practices.

That analysis fed a set of design hypotheses focused on reducing cognitive load, making rules and limits explicit, and reinforcing a sense of control and transparency. I translated the hypotheses into detailed flow diagrams, aligning early with the Product Owner and the design team to validate logic, edge cases, and constraints before moving into visual design. That early alignment reduced rework and ensured usability, compliance, and technical feasibility were resolved from the start.

### Validation and iteration

With the flows defined, I designed mid-fidelity wireframes to validate structure and interaction logic internally. After incorporating feedback, I developed high-fidelity interactive prototypes in two-week cycles, which we then tested with real users in moderated 1:1 sessions, in collaboration with the UX Research team.

We tested with around 50 users across all of Mexico, remotely. Together we defined research questions focused on trust, clarity, and usability:

- How safe did users feel sending money through SPIN?
- How often would they expect to use this flow?
- Could users with low digital literacy complete the task without help?

The result: a 90% acceptance rate. Users consistently described the flow as clear, trustworthy, and easy to understand, regardless of age or level of digital experience.

![spin-sitemap-wireframes.png](/images/projects/oxxo-spin/spin-sitemap-wireframes.png)

> "It feels safe. It puts me at ease." A recurring comment in user testing.

## 04 · Key decisions

**The decisions that don't appear in the brief, but that defined the flow's trust.**

Designing for someone's money is different. A usability error in a notes app is an annoyance. In a transfer, it's the fear of losing what was hard to earn. I made these two decisions on my own, based on experience designing for real users. Both turned out to be central to the success of the testing.

### 01 · Showing the rules and limits before the amount, not after

The common pattern in financial apps is to let the user type the amount and, if it exceeds a limit, show the error afterward. That creates frustration: the person has already settled on sending a certain amount and the system rejects it.

I decided to flip it. The rules, limits, and restrictions are shown before the user types the amount. They know from the start how much they can send and under what conditions. No surprises, no frustrating rejections.

For a user who already distrusts money apps, avoiding a single friction like this completely changes their sense of control.

### 02 · An extra confirmation screen before sending

People's number one fear was making a mistake and losing their money. Not speed. Not the number of steps. The fear of the irreversible error.

I added an explicit confirmation screen before executing the transfer. The user sees who they're sending to, how much, and can review everything before confirming. One more step in the flow, yes. But a step that turns anxiety into reassurance.

The OXXO brand already carried much of that trust. The flow reinforced it. In testing, users mentioned again and again that they felt safe, that the process put them at ease. OXXO's backing opened the door. The design confirmed they could walk through it.

![spin-spei-flow-final.png](/images/projects/oxxo-spin/spin-spei-flow-final.png)

> Sometimes the best design isn't the one that removes steps. It's the one that puts the right step at the right moment.

## 05 · UI and system

**A system-first approach, because in fintech visual consistency is trust.**

With the flows and wireframes validated, I moved into UI design, producing development-ready high-fidelity mockups aligned with SPIN's brand guidelines and the Design System I was building and maintaining at the time.

The UI work was deliberately system-first. Typography, color, spacing, and components that were consistent, reusable, and scalable across the product. In a fintech app this is critical: visual consistency and predictable interactions are part of how user trust is built.

The interface was designed to feel clear, calm, and accessible, especially for users with low digital and financial literacy. I paid special attention to states and feedback (loading, success, error, empty), making sure sensitive actions like SPEI transfers always came with clear visual cues and confirmations.

I built the Design System from scratch. It started small and grew alongside the product during the launch stage. By aligning UI decisions with that evolving system, the team reduced inconsistencies, streamlined developer handoff, and iterated faster. That approach laid the groundwork for SPIN to adapt to regulatory and functional changes over time, preserving a coherent and reliable experience.

![spin-design-system-components.png](/images/projects/oxxo-spin/spin-design-system-components.png)

> The Design System wasn't a side project. It was the infrastructure that made everything else scale.

## 06 · Designing to scale

**From day one, SPIN was designed with the long term in mind.**

SPIN by OXXO was designed from launch with scalability in mind, anticipating the gradual expansion of financial services: remittances, promotions, rewards, and value-added features.

The product was built on modular, flexible flows that allowed new capabilities to be introduced without increasing complexity for the user. Clarity and trust were preserved at every step. A continuous focus on understanding user behavior, especially the non-digitally-native user in Mexico, guided design decisions.

That approach laid the groundwork for SPIN to grow while keeping an accessible and trustworthy experience, even as regulatory requirements and the product offering expanded after my departure.
